This is a collection of [CAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_design) and fused filament fabrication ([FFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fused_filament_fabrication)) resources I find useful.

## CAD Software

### OpenSCAD

[OpenSCAD](https://openscad.org/) — The Programmers Solid 3D CAD Modeller.

Useful libraries:

- [BOSL2](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2) — The Belfry OpenScad Library, v2. Simply put, it's a library of new capabilities for OpenSCAD.
- [YAPP_Box](https://github.com/mrWheel/YAPP_Box) — A Parametric Projectbox generator

### OnShape

[OnShape](https://www.onshape.com/en/) has become my preferred graphical CAD software ever since I moved to Linux everywhere. I used [Autodesk Fusion](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/overview) before that, but getting it to work on Linux was a whole adventure, so I tried OnShape and stuck with it ever since for complex designs.

## 3D printing

- [Design for 3D-Printing by Rahix](https://blog.rahix.de/design-for-3d-printing/) — like every manufacturing process, FFF has its own limitations and oddities. This guide explains how to acommodate them, get the strongest parts, and have a good fabrication experience overall.

### Slicers

[PrusaSlicer](https://www.prusa3d.com/page/prusaslicer_424/) is my current favorite, mostly for its great integration with [PrusaLink](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/prusa-connect-and-prusalink-explained_302608).
